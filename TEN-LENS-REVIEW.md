## A more distinctive learner experience

The eight-unit sequence now asks learners to keep different kinds of evidence, not repeat a generic “How did I do?” question:

| Unit | Communication purpose | Evidence retained by learner |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Repair a misunderstanding | Unclear phrase, revised wording, listener understanding—or an imagined listener question when working alone |
| 2 | Plan and monitor practice | Two attempts, a noticed change and the next adjustment; speed is not the sole progress measure |
| 3 | Clarify before disagreeing | A clarification question, acknowledged perspective and changed phrase, using a fictional situation |
| 4 | Make a reasoned choice | Benefits/trade-offs, a question and the reason to keep or change a fictional-budget recommendation |
| 5 | Qualify a checked claim | Claim, identifiable source and revised explanation, with uncertainty and privacy safeguards |
| 6 | Respect boundaries in disagreement | Both perspectives, an acknowledged boundary and revised wording without personal disclosure |
| 7 | Discuss uneven impacts | Identifiable source, affected people and a qualified claim; missing supply-chain details are not guessed |
| 8 | Adapt for a multilingual listener | A local expression, explanation and revision, judged for clarity rather than accent imitation |

These are newly authored learner tasks, not recovered source text, standardized test descriptors, a certified proficiency scale or independently demonstrated innovations. They make progress more visible without requiring recordings, private disclosures or a paid digital platform.
