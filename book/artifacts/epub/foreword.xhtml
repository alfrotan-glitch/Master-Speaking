Foreword

This book begins with a simple belief: learning English means more than knowing what to say. It means finding a way to express what you think.

As your English develops, you need more than the language of everyday exchanges. You need to explain an idea, question an assumption, tell a story, disagree respectfully, and give reasons for your opinions. You also need to listen when an answer is not simple.

That is what this book is designed to help you do.

The topics in this book connect English with the world around you: relationships, globalization, language, culture, and the choices that shape our future. Readings and listening activities introduce different perspectives. You will discuss them, learn useful expressions, investigate questions, and present ideas of your own.

Some of that work may take you beyond familiar conversations.

A discussion may challenge something you believe. A speaker may make a point you disagree with. You do not have to accept every argument you encounter; you do need to understand it before you respond.

Your English classroom should be a place where you can think out loud.

You may hesitate, make a mistake, or change your mind. You may hear a stronger argument or find yourself considering a view you had not taken seriously before.

Use those moments to ask a better question, revise an explanation, or try a different way of making your point.

Take an active part in your own progress. Prepare, but do not wait for someone to give you every word. Leave room to think while you speak. After a conversation, notice one thing that worked and one thing to try differently.

Give yourself opportunities to use English, listen to feedback, and try again.

The journey starts here.

Turn the page—and let’s begin.

Abdul Raziq Nazari
