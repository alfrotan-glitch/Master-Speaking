How to Use This Book

A Practical Guide for Students

Welcome to Master Speaking, a course from The TOEFL House.

Use this book to practise explaining, questioning, discussing, and presenting ideas in English.

Each unit gives you several ways to explore a topic. You are not expected to produce a perfect answer. Prepare your ideas, speak in your own words, and use feedback to make the next attempt clearer.

Prepare a few key words, speak in your own words, listen for your partner’s main point, and ask a follow-up question. After feedback, repeat part of the task and notice one improvement. Aim for clear, thoughtful communication rather than memorized answers or imitation of a particular accent.

For personal or sensitive questions, you may use a fictional example or discuss a comparable team or community situation. You do not need to disclose private information. Respect other perspectives and ask permission before recording anyone.

When presenting research, identify the author or organization, the source title, and the date or page where available. Explain what the evidence supports, distinguish facts from opinions, and acknowledge uncertainty. Do not invent statistics or references.

How Is Each Unit Organized?

Each unit is divided into four chapters.

Chapter 1 — Speaking through Reading

Explore the unit’s topic through a reading, vocabulary, questions, and speaking activities.

  • activate your background knowledge
  • learn useful topic-related language
  • discuss general questions
  • use personal or fictional examples
  • express and defend your opinions

Chapter 2 — Speaking through Listening

Listen to a fictional story read aloud by a teacher or partner. Practise identifying its main point and details, then discuss the wider topic. The script is at the end of each listening chapter for checking after the task.

  • listen for a main point and supporting details
  • check your understanding against the story
  • use topic vocabulary in a discussion
  • ask for clarification and compare interpretations
  • retell a situation in your own words

Chapter 3 — Speaking through Discussion

Use the language from the reading and listening lessons to develop your own ideas.

  • use collocations and phrasal verbs
  • practice discussion expressions
  • activate topic vocabulary in conversation
  • take part in group discussions
  • compare and rank ideas
  • answer challenging questions
  • speak without a full script

Chapter 4 — Speaking through Research Presentation

Develop a focused research question and present an answer supported by identifiable sources.

  • practice a pronunciation skill
  • learn presentation expressions
  • research a question
  • organize your ideas
  • use evidence and examples
  • prepare a short presentation
  • respond to questions from your classmates

At the end of a unit, return to its main question. Explain your position, support it with reasons or evidence, and notice one thing you can now communicate more clearly.
