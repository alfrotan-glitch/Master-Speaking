Pronunciation Clinic

Skill: Sentence Stress

Speakers often emphasize key content words and give less stress to other words. Stress depends on meaning: even a short grammar word can be important when you want to make a contrast. Choose the words that help your listener understand your message.

Compare

I LIKE TO SPEAK ENGLISH WITH MY FRIENDS EVERY DAY.

Equal stress on every word can make the main message harder to follow.

I like to SPEAK ENGLISH with my FRIENDS every DAY.

Practice

Read these sentences aloud. Choose the key words to stress so that your meaning is clear.

Communication is an essential life skill.

Good listeners ask follow-up questions.

Practice builds confidence.

Great speakers always respect their audience.

Clear communication helps people solve problems.

Rules

Speak for 3–4 minutes.

Do not read your paper word for word.

Maintain eye contact.

Aim for a clear message and a comfortable pace; pause when the audience needs time to follow.

Use at least five expressions from the Presentation Toolbox.

Use at least five collocations from the listening or discussion chapter in this unit.

Sources Used

List the author or organization, title, and date for each source. Add a page number or link when available.

Reflect and Try Again

Ask a partner: Was my main point clear? Did my evidence support it? Where did you need clarification? Revise one passage and say it again.

Check your progress: Can you explain your main point, listen to a response, and repair a misunderstanding without starting the whole conversation again?

Put it to use: Explain a useful idea to a partner, then ask them to tell you what they understood. Clarify one detail and try that part again. Keep a progress note: the unclear phrase, your revised wording, and what your partner understood the second time. If you are working alone, write a possible listener question and answer it aloud.
