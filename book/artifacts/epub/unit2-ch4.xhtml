Speaking through Research Presentation

Pronunciation Clinic

Skill: Thought Groups & Strategic Pausing

Professional speakers do not speak in one long stream. Instead, they divide their ideas into thought groups, pausing briefly between them. This makes their speech easier to understand and gives them time to think.

Example

✗ Poor Delivery

Success depends on good habits and everyone should build better routines because habits determine our future.

✔ Better Delivery

Success depends on good habits // and everyone should build better routines // because habits determine our future.

(// = short pause)

Practice

Read aloud, using the marked pauses as a starting point. Try another grouping and notice how it changes the emphasis.

Setting meaningful objectives // requires patience // persistence // and self-discipline.

Many successful people // focus on their daily systems // rather than short-term goals.

Failure should not discourage us // because every setback // provides an opportunity to learn.

Research Task

Topic

Goals or Systems: Which Leads to Greater Success?

Investigate your question before presenting. Keep notes linking each claim to its source.

Use at least three sources. Check the author’s relevant knowledge, the evidence, the date, and the purpose; a familiar website or a successful person is not automatically reliable.

Guiding Questions

  1. What is the difference between a goal and a system?
  2. Why do some experts believe systems are more effective than goals?
  3. Can goals ever become harmful?
  4. What habits do highly successful people have?
  5. Which approach do you personally believe is more effective? Why?

Organizing Your Presentation

Prepare a four- to five-minute presentation using the outline. Select evidence that answers your question and practise keeping within the time.

Introduction

Introduce your topic.

Explain why it is important.

Body

Explain the difference between goals and systems.

Present at least two findings from identifiable sources. Explain what they support and any limits you notice.

Give one identifiable real-life example. Explain how it supports your point.

Explain your own opinion.

Conclusion

Summarize your main points.

Leave the audience with one final message.

Sources Used

List the author or organization, title, and date for each source. Add a page number or link when available.

Reflect and Try Again

Ask a partner: Was my main point clear? Did my evidence support it? Where did you need clarification? Revise one passage and say it again.

Check your progress: Can you explain a realistic goal, the habit that supports it, and a sensible change to make when your plan is not working?

Put it to use: Choose one small speaking habit to try this week. Decide when you will practise and what you will look for, such as giving a clear example or asking a follow-up question. After two attempts, keep a progress note: what you tried, one change you noticed, and the next adjustment you will make. Progress need not mean speaking faster.
