Work with a teacher or partner. Keep the script at the end of this chapter covered until after the second listening.

Listening Strategy

First Listening

Listen to the whole story without reading the script. What is the main problem or decision? Give a one-sentence answer.

Second Listening

Read the detail questions, listen again, and take short notes in a notebook under the two headings below. Ask to hear a part again if you need it.

Main PointDetails I Heard

Listening Comprehension

Answer in short phrases. Use details you heard, not guesses about what usually happens.

  1. What did the speaker first assume about Kareem?
  2. Did Kareem understand the task?
  3. What made it difficult for him to contribute?
  4. What change did the group make?
  5. What did Kareem contribute?

Compare and Clarify

Compare answers with a partner. Explain which detail supports an answer. Check the script only after the listening task, then correct any misunderstanding.

Group Discussion

Use the story as a starting point for the wider questions below. Distinguish what the narrator describes from your own opinions and predictions.

Discuss the questions in groups of three or four. Give everyone a turn and ask for clarification when a point is unclear.

  1. What could the narrator do before speaking if they felt frustrated with Kareem?
  2. How might noticing your emotions help you respond to a disagreement? Give an example.
  3. What are two healthy strategies for regulating emotions after an emotional response?
  4. Why can sadness sometimes be a helpful emotion?
  5. Which response in the story would be useful in a group you know? Explain why.

Think Beyond

  1. Do you agree that there are no “good” or “bad” emotions, only helpful or unhelpful ones? Why or why not?
  2. Should schools teach students how to regulate their emotions as part of the curriculum? Give reasons.
  3. In your opinion, is emotional intelligence more important than academic intelligence in today’s workplace? Explain.
  4. Some people believe we should always stay positive. Others believe we should accept every emotion we feel. Which view do you agree with? Why?

Challenge Question

Consider this view: trying to feel happy all the time can make ordinary disappointments harder to accept.

  1. Do you agree or disagree? Support your opinion with examples from your own experience or from people you know.

Try It Again

Retell the main problem and response in your own words. Ask a partner one follow-up question, then make your retelling clearer.

Listening Script

Before making an assumption

Fictional story • read aloud by a teacher or partner

During our group project, Kareem spoke very little. At first, I thought he was not interested. I nearly told the teacher that he was not helping. Instead, I asked him privately whether the task was clear. He said he understood it, but the group moved so quickly that he could not find a moment to speak. He had already written several useful suggestions in his notebook. At the next meeting, we gave everyone a minute to explain one idea without interruption. Kareem suggested a simpler way to organize our presentation, and we used it. I learned that silence did not tell me enough about his effort or interest. Asking a question gave me information that my first assumption had missed.

For independent reading practice, read the story once, cover it, and answer from memory. This is reading recall; it does not replace listening to another person.
