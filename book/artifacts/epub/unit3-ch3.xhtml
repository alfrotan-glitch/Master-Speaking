Speaking Toolbox

Study 10 carefully selected collocations, phrasal verbs, and discussion expressions related to the week’s topic.

ExpressionMeaningExample
keep your emotions in checkcontrol your emotions in a difficult situationA good leader knows how to keep their emotions in check during a crisis.

Guided Discussion

Work in groups of 3–4 students. Use at least eight expressions from the Speaking Toolbox.

Discuss the following questions with your group. Use the expressions from the Speaking Toolbox whenever possible.

  1. Why do some people lose their temper more easily than others?
  2. Why is it important to see things from someone else’s perspective before making a judgment?
  3. How can people respond in a constructive way during disagreements?
  4. What are some ways to build mutual trust in friendships or at work?
  5. Why do emotionally intelligent people avoid jumping to conclusions?

Mini Debate

Choose one statement. Work in two teams. One team agrees, and the other disagrees. Give reasons and examples to support your opinions.

Debate 1

“Emotional intelligence is more important than IQ in the workplace.”

Debate 2

“People should always control their emotions, even in very difficult situations.”

Debate 3

“Learning to accept criticism is one of the most important life skills.”

Speaking Challenge

The Emotional Intelligence Coach

Work in pairs.

Student A describes a real or imaginary situation involving a conflict, disappointment, or stressful event.

Examples:

  • Failing an important exam
  • Losing a job opportunity
  • Arguing with a close friend
  • Receiving unfair criticism
  • Feeling nervous before a presentation

Student B acts as an Emotional Intelligence Coach. Give advice using at least six expressions from the Speaking Toolbox. Explain how the person can manage their emotions, communicate effectively, and solve the problem.

After three minutes, switch roles.
