Speaking through Research Presentation

Pronunciation Clinic

Skill: Using Effective Intonation

Good presenters don’t speak with the same tone all the time. They use intonation to keep the audience interested and to make important ideas stand out.

Use Rising Intonation (↗)

Wh-questions often end with a falling tone. A rising tone can invite a response or show uncertainty; here, practise asking the questions with a curious, tentative tone. Intonation depends on your meaning and the situation.

Examples

↗ Why do some people manage emotions better than others?

↗ What would you do in this situation?

Use Falling Intonation (↘)

A falling tone can suit these purposes; the meaning and situation still matter:

finishing a statement

giving facts

expressing confidence

Examples

↘ Emotional intelligence can be developed through practice.

↘ This strategy improves communication.

Emphasize Key Words

Read the first version with equal stress on each word. In the second version, give extra stress to the words in capitals. Notice how the key message becomes clearer.

Flat delivery

Emotional intelligence is important for everyone.

With key-word stress

Emotional intelligence is IMPORTANT for EVERYONE.

Practice

Choose an intention for each sentence, then read it aloud. Try a different tone and explain how the meaning changes.

  1. Why is emotional intelligence important in leadership?
  2. Emotional intelligence helps people build stronger relationships.
  3. What would happen if people never controlled their emotions?
  4. We can improve emotional intelligence through daily practice.

Research Task

Topic

Can Emotional Intelligence Be Improved?

Research the following questions.

What is emotional intelligence?

Why is it important?

Can emotional intelligence be learned?

What habits help people improve emotional intelligence?

How does emotional intelligence affect success at school or work?

Organizing Your Presentation

Prepare a four- to five-minute presentation using the outline. Select evidence that answers your question and practise keeping within the time.

Introduction

Introduce your topic.

Explain why it is important.

Body

Explain what emotional intelligence means and assess the evidence for improving it.

Present at least two findings from identifiable sources. Explain what they support and any limits you notice.

Give one identifiable real-life example. Explain how it supports your point.

Explain your own opinion.

Conclusion

Summarize your main points.

Leave the audience with one final message.

Sources Used

List the author or organization, title, and date for each source. Add a page number or link when available.

Reflect and Try Again

Ask a partner: Was my main point clear? Did my evidence support it? Where did you need clarification? Revise one passage and say it again.

Check your progress: Can you describe an emotional response, consider another perspective, and suggest a constructive way to continue a difficult conversation?

Put it to use: Use a fictional disagreement. Give a response that asks for clarification, then explain the other person’s perspective before suggesting a next step. Try again after feedback or your own reflection. Keep a progress note: your clarification question, the perspective you acknowledged, and one phrase you changed. You do not need to share a personal conflict.
