Listening Strategy

First Viewing

✔ Do not write.

✔ Listen for the main idea.

Ask yourself:

  1. What is the speaker trying to convince us to do?
Second Viewing

Take notes.

Focus on key ideas, examples, reasons. Do not try to write every word.

Listening Comprehension

A. Multiple Choice

Choose the best answer.

  1. What is the main problem with giving an AI system a fixed objective?
    1. a. The AI may be unable to complete the task.
    2. b. The AI may pursue the objective without considering other things humans care about.
    3. c. The AI may refuse to follow instructions.
  2. What example does Stuart Russell use to explain the problem with fixed objectives?
    1. a. An AI system trying to make coffee.
    2. b. An AI system trying to fix ocean acidification but causing other environmental problems.
    3. c. An AI system trying to teach people about climate change.
  3. Why can humans deal with objectives differently from current AI systems?
    1. a. Humans understand that they may not know everything that matters.
    2. b. Humans always make better decisions than machines.
    3. c. Humans have more information than AI systems.
  4. What does the speaker mean by technological unemployment?
    1. a. People refusing to use modern technology.
    2. b. Unemployment caused by machines performing work previously done by humans.
    3. c. Companies losing money because of technology.
  5. What concern does the speaker raise about a machine-dependent civilization?
    1. a. People may lose the incentive to understand and manage their own civilization.
    2. b. Machines may become too expensive to maintain.
    3. c. People may stop communicating with one another.

B. True or False

Write T (True) or F (False). Correct the false statements.

  • _____ The speaker believes that humans can always predict every possible consequence of an AI objective.
  • _____ AI systems may need to recognize that they do not fully understand the objectives humans have in mind.
  • _____ The speaker suggests that automation has the potential to eliminate millions of jobs.
  • _____ According to the speaker, general-purpose AI will arrive suddenly on one specific date.
  • _____ The speaker believes that the development of general-purpose AI is difficult to predict precisely.

Group Discussion

Work in groups of 3–4 students. Discuss the following questions.

  1. Which idea from the video did you find most interesting? Why?
  2. What potential benefit of AI do you think could have the greatest impact on society?
  3. What potential risk concerns you the most?
  4. Do you think humans currently understand AI well enough to control it?
  5. What does it mean for technology to be human-compatible?
Think Beyond
  1. Would you feel comfortable using an AI system to make an important decision for you? Why or why not?
  2. Do you think AI will create more jobs than it destroys, or destroy more jobs than it creates?
  3. Should governments pose restrictions on the development of powerful AI systems?
  4. Are people taking AI’s benefits for granted? Explain.
  5. If AI becomes much more intelligent than humans, should humans still have the final decision-making power?

Imagine that scientists develop an AI system that can solve major problems such as climate change.

  1. What could happen if an AI system achieves its goals but does not understand human values?
  2. Who should be responsible if an AI system causes serious harm—the developers, the company, the users, or the AI itself?
  3. Do you think humanity is prepared for the changes AI may bring? Why or why not?
Challenge Question
