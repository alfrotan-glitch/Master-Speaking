Listening Strategy

First Listening

Listen to the whole story without reading the script. What is the main problem or decision? Give a one-sentence answer.

Second Listening

Read the detail questions, listen again, and take short notes in a notebook under the two headings below. Ask to hear a part again if you need it.

Main PointDetails I Heard

Listening Comprehension

Answer in short phrases. Use details you heard, not guesses about what usually happens.

  1. Which day did the tool invent?
  2. What were the verified day and room?
  3. Where did the speaker first check the meeting details?
  4. What part of the draft did the speaker keep?
  5. What private information was kept out of the tool?

Compare and Clarify

Compare answers with a partner. Explain which detail supports an answer. Check the script only after the listening task, then correct any misunderstanding.

Group Discussion

Use the story as a starting point for the wider questions below. Distinguish what the narrator describes from your own opinions and predictions.

Discuss the questions in groups of three or four. Give everyone a turn and ask for clarification when a point is unclear.

  1. Which decision in the story did you find most useful to discuss? Why?
  2. What potential benefit of AI do you think could have the greatest impact on society?
  3. What potential risk concerns you the most?
  4. Do you think humans currently understand AI well enough to control it?
  5. What does it mean for technology to be human-compatible?

Think Beyond

  1. Would you feel comfortable using an AI system to make an important decision for you? Why or why not?
  2. Do you think AI will create more jobs than it destroys, or destroy more jobs than it creates?
  3. Should governments impose restrictions on the development of powerful AI systems?
  4. Are people taking AI’s benefits for granted? Explain.
  5. If AI becomes much more intelligent than humans, should humans still have the final decision-making power?
  6. Imagine that scientists develop an AI system that can solve major problems such as climate change, disease, and poverty. Should we give it significant decision-making power?
  7. What could happen if an AI system achieves its goals but does not understand human values?
  8. Who should be responsible if an AI system causes serious harm—the developers, the company, the users, or the AI itself?
  9. Do you think humanity is prepared for the changes AI may bring? Why or why not?

Challenge Question

  1. If AI becomes more intelligent than humans, should we see it as our greatest opportunity or our greatest threat?

Choose a position and support it with at least three arguments. Explain the assumptions behind any prediction.

Try It Again

Retell the main problem and response in your own words. Ask a partner one follow-up question, then make your retelling clearer.

Listening Script

A confident answer was not enough

Fictional story • read aloud by a teacher or partner

I asked an AI tool to help me draft an announcement for our club meeting. It produced a clear paragraph and said we would meet on Thursday in Room Three. I had not given it those details. Before sharing the announcement, I checked the noticeboard. The actual meeting was on Wednesday in Room Five. I corrected the day and room, then asked the organizer to check the whole message. I kept the tool’s suggested order of information because it was useful, but I did not trust the invented details. I also left classmates’ phone numbers out of the tool. When I shared the final announcement, I explained that a tool had helped with the draft and that the organizer had confirmed the meeting information.

For independent reading practice, read the story once, cover it, and answer from memory. This is reading recall; it does not replace listening to another person.
