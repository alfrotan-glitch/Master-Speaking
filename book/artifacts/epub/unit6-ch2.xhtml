Listening Strategy

First Listening

Listen to the whole story without reading the script. What is the main problem or decision? Give a one-sentence answer.

Second Listening

Read the detail questions, listen again, and take short notes in a notebook under the two headings below. Ask to hear a part again if you need it.

Main PointDetails I Heard

Listening Comprehension

Answer in short phrases. Use details you heard, not guesses about what usually happens.

  1. How often did Mariam first expect to meet?
  2. Why could Salim help only monthly?
  3. What schedule did they agree to try?
  4. Who would arrange the room?
  5. Which question remained unresolved?

Compare and Clarify

Compare answers with a partner. Explain which detail supports an answer. Check the script only after the listening task, then correct any misunderstanding.

Group Discussion

Use the story as a starting point for the wider questions below. Distinguish what the narrator describes from your own opinions and predictions.

Discuss the questions in groups of three or four. Give everyone a turn and ask for clarification when a point is unclear.

  1. Why are small promises important for building trust?
  2. Is trust easy to rebuild after it has been broken?
  3. What is the difference between empathy and compassion?
  4. Can being too emotionally involved in your partner’s problems become unhealthy?
  5. What does it really mean to “show up” for someone?
  6. Should married couples have the same long-term goals?
  7. What happens when two partners have completely different ideas about their future?
  8. Is compromise healthier than sacrifice? Why?
  9. Do loving behaviors become less important after people have been together for many years?

Challenge Question

  1. Can everyday actions matter more than a declaration of love? Explain your view with examples, and consider a different view.

Try It Again

Retell the main problem and response in your own words. Ask a partner one follow-up question, then make your retelling clearer.

Listening Script

Friends with different plans

Fictional story • read aloud by a teacher or partner

Two friends, Mariam and Salim, offered to run a community reading club. They both liked the idea, but they had not discussed the work involved. Mariam expected to meet every week. Salim could help only once a month because of his work schedule. Their first conversation became tense because each thought the other had changed the plan. When they compared their notes, they realized they had never agreed on a schedule. They decided to hold one meeting a month for the first three months. Mariam would choose a short reading, and Salim would arrange the room. They would share the task of welcoming new members. They still needed to decide how to pay for printing, so they wrote that question down for their next conversation.

For independent reading practice, read the story once, cover it, and answer from memory. This is reading recall; it does not replace listening to another person.
