Speaking Toolbox

  1. What Makes Relationships Work?

Study 10 carefully selected collocations, phrasal verbs, and discussion expressions related to the week’s topic.

Vocabulary / CollocationMeaningExample
maintain a relationshipto keep a relationship strong and healthyIt takes effort to maintain a healthy relationship over many years.
long-term commitmenta serious decision to remain dedicated to someone or something for a long timeMarriage usually involves a long-term commitment.
resolve a conflictto find a solution to a disagreementCouples need to learn how to resolve conflicts respectfully.
compromise on somethingto accept less than what you originally wanted in order to reach an agreementThey had to compromise on where they would live.
have conflicting goalsto have goals that cannot easily be achieved togetherThey began to realize that they had conflicting goals for the future.
rebuild trustto gradually restore confidence after it has been damagedIt can take years to rebuild trust after betrayal.
set boundariesto clearly communicate what behavior you will or will not acceptHealthy couples should be able to set boundaries without threatening each other.
emotional distancea lack of emotional closeness between peopleThey still lived together, but emotional distance had developed between them.
deal with disagreementto manage a difference of opinionMature couples learn how to deal with disagreement without becoming disrespectful.
mutual understandinga situation in which two people understand and respect each other’s feelings or opinionsMutual understanding is essential when partners have different priorities.

Vocabulary Activation

Work in pairs. Choose 10 expressions and create a short conversation using all ten.

Guided Discussion

Work in groups of 3–4 students. Use at least eight expressions from the Speaking Toolbox.

Discussion Questions

  1. Do you think attraction naturally changes as people grow older?
  2. Can emotional attraction be stronger than physical attraction?
  3. Is it possible to love someone deeply without being attracted to them?
  4. Why do some people confuse the end of excitement with the end of love?
  5. What should couples do when the relationship becomes less exciting?
  6. Do you think people’s expectations of marriage are becoming unrealistic?
  7. Is financial stability necessary for a successful marriage?
  8. Should married couples have completely shared goals?
  9. How might technology affect marriage in the future?
Challenge Question
  1. If two people love each other but their long-term goals are completely incompatible, should they stay together or separate? Defend your position.

Speaking Challenge 1
