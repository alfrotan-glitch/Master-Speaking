Listening Strategy

First Listening

Listen to the whole story without reading the script. What is the main problem or decision? Give a one-sentence answer.

Second Listening

Read the detail questions, listen again, and take short notes in a notebook under the two headings below. Ask to hear a part again if you need it.

Main PointDetails I Heard

Listening Comprehension

Answer in short phrases. Use details you heard, not guesses about what usually happens.

  1. How many sellers were invited?
  2. What cost worried the organizers?
  3. How long would the local collection trial last?
  4. How could sellers without reliable internet get help?
  5. Had they already decided to expand the market?

Compare and Clarify

Compare answers with a partner. Explain which detail supports an answer. Check the script only after the listening task, then correct any misunderstanding.

Listen More Closely

Separate what the account states from what you infer. Support an interpretation with a detail; consider another reasonable reading.

  1. Why would counting orders alone give an incomplete picture of the trial?
  2. What would make consultation meaningful rather than merely symbolic?

Group Discussion

Use the story as a starting point for the wider questions below. Distinguish what the narrator describes from your own opinions and predictions.

Discuss the questions in groups of three or four. Give everyone a turn and ask for clarification when a point is unclear.

  1. Should advanced medical and genetic technologies be available to everyone?
  2. If technology allows people to live much longer, how might society change?
  3. Would longer life expectancy be a blessing or a problem?
  4. Do you agree that migration will become more important in the future?
  5. Which risk associated with an online market would you examine before expanding it?
  6. Do you think individual countries can solve global problems on their own?
  7. Why might a group make a better decision after hearing from people who could not take part?

Challenge Question

  1. If you could give one piece of advice to the world’s leaders about the future, what would it be?

Try It Again

Retell the main problem and response in your own words. Ask a partner one follow-up question, then make your retelling clearer.

Listening Script

Testing an online market

Fictional story • read aloud by a teacher or partner

A community center invited six craft sellers to try an online market. Some sellers hoped to reach customers in other countries, but the organizers were worried about shipping costs. They decided to begin with local collection for one month. Sellers without reliable internet could bring product descriptions to the center, where a volunteer would help prepare listings with their permission. The group agreed to record the number of orders, the costs, and the time each seller spent on the trial. They also planned to ask people who could not take part what had stopped them. No one knew yet whether the market would be worth continuing. At the end of the month, they would review the information together before deciding whether to expand.

One organizer wanted to announce success as soon as the first orders arrived. Another asked whose costs would appear in that announcement. A volunteer might spend an hour preparing a listing, while a seller might wait all afternoon for a collection. They decided that the review should include those experiences, not just sales. They would also ask whether the trial had brought in new customers or merely moved existing orders online. The smaller trial postponed the possibility of wider trade, but it made a mistake less expensive to correct. At the review, sellers would be able to recommend changing the plan, continuing it, or stopping. An invitation to participate would not count as a voice in the decision unless their views could affect what happened next.

For independent reading practice, read the story once, cover it, and answer from memory. This is reading recall; it does not replace listening to another person.
