Speaking through Research Presentation

Pronunciation Clinic

Skill: The /θ/ and /ð/ Sounds: “th” Is Not Always the Same

English th represents two different sounds:

/θ/ — voiceless TH

Your vocal cords do not vibrate.

think

Examples:

  • think
  • threat
  • three
  • growth

/ð/ — voiced TH

Your vocal cords vibrate.

these (theez)

Examples:

  • these
  • those
  • the
  • they
  • although
  • other

Quick Test

Touch your throat lightly. Sustain /θ/ and then /ð/ without adding a vowel; compare the vibration before saying the example words.

Say:

  • think — this

You should feel no vibration with /θ/ but vibration with /ð/.

Common Problem

Students sometimes pronounce:

  • think → sink
  • three → tree
  • this → dis
  • those → dose

Mouth Position

Put the tip of your tongue lightly between your teeth.

Don’t bite your tongue.

Blow air gently for /θ/.

Add your voice for /ð/.

Practice

Read each pair and notice which sound is voiced. Keep the vowel separate from the consonant contrast.

  • think — this
  • three — these
  • threat — that
  • thought — though
  • through — those

Then read:

These three things threaten the future of globalization.

Although technology creates opportunities, there are also threats.

Challenge

Say:

  • “I think these three things could change the future.”

Start slowly, then use a comfortable speaking pace without losing the sound contrast.

Research Task

Research your topic using reliable sources.

Investigate the question below. Use the guiding questions to plan your research.

Globalization: Opportunity or Threat?

Research your topic and answer:

  1. What are the major benefits of globalization?
  2. What are its major disadvantages?
  3. Who benefits most from globalization?
  4. Who may be left out?
  5. How has technology accelerated globalization?
  6. How can globalization affect local cultures?
  7. How can globalization contribute to inequality?
  8. What can governments do to make globalization more inclusive?
  9. What role should individuals play?
  10. What kind of global future do you want to see?

Reflect and Try Again

Ask a partner: Was my main point clear? Did my evidence support it? Where did you need clarification? Revise one passage and say it again.

Check your progress: Can you explain a global connection, identify who may benefit or lose, and support a judgment without ignoring a trade-off?

Put it to use: Choose an everyday product or service with an international connection. Use an identifiable source, including a teacher-provided text, to investigate it. Explain one possible benefit, one possible cost, and one unanswered question. Keep a progress note: your source, who may be affected, and one claim you qualified after checking.
