Focus on these useful expressions based on the TED-Ed lesson.

Vocabulary / ExpressionMeaningExample
language lossthe disappearance of a languageLanguage loss can also mean the loss of cultural knowledge.
Unprecedentednever happening before or happening at an unusually high levelLanguages are dying at an unprecedented rate.
morph intoto gradually change into something differentEnglish teaching has morphed into a massive international business.
Bandwagona popular activity that many people join because it is becoming successfulEnglish education has become a global bandwagon.
linguistic abilitysomeone’s ability to use or understand a languageA person’s linguistic ability does not necessarily show their intelligence.
Gatekeepera person or system that controls access to somethingEnglish teachers can become gatekeepers to higher education.
self-fulfilling prophecya belief or expectation that helps cause itself to become trueThe English requirement can become a self-fulfilling prophecy because more research is then produced in English.
Arbitrarybased on a choice rather than a clear logical reasonShe argues that equating intelligence with English knowledge is arbitrary.

Listening Strategy

First Viewing

✔ Listen for the main idea.

Ask yourself:

Second Viewing

Take notes.

Listening Comprehension

A. Multiple Choice

Choose the best answer.

  1. What did Ryan’s friend’s students teach her in the garden?
    1. A. How to grow vegetables
    2. B. Arabic words for local plants and their uses
    3. C. How to teach English vocabulary
    4. D. Traditional English medicine
  2. Where did the students get their knowledge about the plants?
    1. A. From their teachers
    2. B. From books
    3. C. From their grandparents and great-grandparents
    4. D. From the Internet
  3. According to Ryan, what has English teaching become?
    1. A. A small school subject
    2. B. A declining profession
    3. C. A massive international business
    4. D. A completely unnecessary subject
  4. Why do many people want an English education?
    1. A. They believe it gives them access to better educational and professional opportunities.
    2. B. They want to travel to Britain.
    3. C. They want to become English teachers.
    4. D. They want to replace their native languages.
  5. Why does Ryan criticize some English-language requirements?
    1. A. They are too easy.
    2. B. They can prevent talented people from pursuing their dreams.
    3. C. They are only used in the UK.
    4. D. They do not test grammar.
  6. What does Ryan mean when she calls English teachers “gatekeepers”?
    1. A. They protect English from other languages.
    2. B. They decide whether students meet the language requirements for certain opportunities.
    3. C. They control international universities.
    4. D. They teach students how to pass through airports.
  7. Why does Ryan mention the Islamic Golden Age?
    1. A. To show the historical importance of translation
    2. B. To explain where English originated
    3. C. To criticize Arabic education
    4. D. To discuss the history of universities
  8. Why does Ryan mention Einstein?
    1. A. To show that Einstein was fluent in English
    2. B. To show that brilliant people did not always need to pass English tests to contribute to knowledge
    3. C. To explain how TOEFL was created
    4. D. To demonstrate the importance of English pronunciation
  9. What problem does Ryan identify with English tests?
    1. A. They are too short.
    2. B. They are difficult for native speakers.
    3. C. Their fees can be prohibitive for poor people.
    4. D. They test too many languages.
  10. What is Ryan’s overall attitude toward English?
    1. A. She believes English should disappear.
    2. B. She is completely against teaching English.
    3. C. She supports English as a global language but opposes using it as a barrier.
    4. D. She believes everyone should speak only English.

Group Discussion

Work in groups of 3–4 students. Discuss the following questions.

  1. What knowledge exists in Afghanistan that is mainly passed down through local languages?
  2. What could be lost if a local language disappears?
  3. Do Afghan students sometimes feel that English ability = intelligence?
  4. Can someone be an excellent doctor, engineer, teacher, farmer, or businessperson without speaking excellent English?
  5. Has English opened opportunities for young Afghans? How?
  6. Can English also create inequality between people who have access to English education and those who don’t?

Mini Debate

“English proficiency should NOT be a requirement for university admission.”

Split the class into two groups.

Group A — Agree

Prepare 3 arguments supporting the statement.

Group B — Disagree

Prepare 3 arguments against it.
