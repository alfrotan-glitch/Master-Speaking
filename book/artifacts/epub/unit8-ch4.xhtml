Pronunciation Clinic

Skill: The Schwa /ə/ — Making Unstressed Vowels Natural

One of the biggest differences between natural English pronunciation and careful learner pronunciation is the schwa /ə/.

The schwa is the weak, relaxed vowel sound found in many unstressed syllables. Learning to use it makes speech smoother, faster, and more natural—especially during presentations.

  1. The Schwa in Common Words

see how the unstressed vowel becomes weak:

about → /əˈbaʊt/

around → /əˈraʊnd/

support → /səˈpɔːrt/

today → /təˈdeɪ/

ago → /əˈɡoʊ/

perhaps → /pərˈhæps/

The first vowel in about is not pronounced strongly like the “a” in cat. It is relaxed: about → ə-BOUT

  1. The Schwa in Academic Vocabulary

These words are especially useful for today’s presentation topic:

globalization → /ˌɡləʊbəlaɪˈzeɪʃən/

communication → /kəˌmjuːnɪˈkeɪʃən/

international → /ˌɪntəˈnæʃənəl/

education → /ˌedʒəˈkeɪʃən/

linguistic → /lɪŋˈɡwɪstɪk/

professional → /prəˈfeʃənəl/

cultural → /ˈkʌltʃərəl/

different → /ˈdɪfrənt/

Students should notice that unstressed vowels are often reduced, rather than pronounced as clearly as stressed vowels.

  1. The Schwa in Function Words

The schwa is particularly common in weak forms:

to → /tə/a → /ə/the → /ðə/for → /fə/of → /əv/ or /ə/can → /kən/

Compare:

I want to learn English.

Natural speech:

I want tə learn English.

And:

English is a global language.

Natural speech:

English is ə global language.

The purpose is not to speak unclearly. The purpose is to make the important words stand out naturally.

Quick Practice

Ask students to underline the syllables that should contain a schwa /ə/.

about

international

communication

education

professional

globalization

cultural

support

Then have students say the words first carefully, and then naturally.

Research Task

Research your topic using reliable sources.

Choose one of the following topics.

Research your topic using reliable sources.

Choose ONE of the following questions.

1. Is English Really a Global Language?

  1. Why has English become an international language?
  2. Why do people from different countries use English to communicate?
  3. What are the advantages and disadvantages of having one global language?

2. Who Owns English?

  1. Should English belong to native speakers?
  2. What happens when millions of non-native speakers use and change English?
  3. Should there be one “correct” form of international English?

3. Does English Proficiency Equal Intelligence?

  1. Why do people sometimes associate English ability with intelligence?
  2. Can someone be highly intelligent but have weak English?
  3. Should universities judge academic ability separately from language ability?

4. English as a Barrier to Education

  1. Can English-language requirements prevent talented students from studying abroad?
  2. Are English tests always fair?
  3. How could universities evaluate students more fairly?

5. The Future of English

  1. How might English change over the next 30 years?
  2. Will English continue to be the world’s main international language?
  3. Could another language eventually become more influential?

6. Language Diversity and Globalization

  1. Why are some languages disappearing?
  2. What is lost when a language disappears?
  3. Can globalization and language diversity exist together?
