Foreword

This book was created with one simple belief: learning English is not only about learning what to say—it is about learning how to express what you think.

As your English develops, your conversations should become more than exchanges of simple information. You should be able to explain your ideas, question assumptions, disagree respectfully, tell meaningful stories, defend your opinions, and engage with ideas that do not have simple answers.

That is what this book is designed to help you do.

Throughout the book, you will encounter topics that are connected to the world around you: relationships, globalization, language, society, culture, and the challenges that shape our future. You will listen to authentic speakers, encounter different perspectives, learn useful expressions, discuss difficult questions, conduct research, and present your own ideas.

You will also be asked to step outside your comfort zone.

Some questions will have no single correct answer. Some discussions may challenge what you already believe. Some videos may introduce ideas you disagree with. That is intentional.

Your English classroom should be a place where you can think out loud.

You may make mistakes. You may change your opinion. You may discover that someone else’s argument is stronger than yours. You may even find yourself defending an idea you had never considered before.

All of these are signs that you are learning.

This book also asks you to take responsibility for your own progress. Don’t wait for your teacher to give you every word. Don’t memorize every answer. Don’t be afraid of silence while you search for the right expression.

Your English will improve as you use it.

The journey starts here.

Turn the page—and let’s begin.

The Power of Communication

Imagine living for one day without communication. You cannot ask questions, explain your ideas, express your feelings, or understand other people. Daily activities such as buying food, attending school, making friends, or working with colleagues would become extremely difficult. Communication is not simply a school subject; it is one of the most important life skills.

Many people believe that communication means speaking well. In reality, communication is much broader than speaking. It includes listening carefully, asking meaningful questions, understanding body language, writing clearly, and responding appropriately. Great communicators know that communication is a two-way process. They do not only speak; they also listen with attention and respect.

Unfortunately, many English learners believe they should remain silent until their English becomes perfect. This belief often prevents them from making progress. Language experts agree that mistakes are a natural part of learning. Every conversation provides an opportunity to improve vocabulary, pronunciation, and confidence.

Consider two students. Ali knows thousands of English words but rarely speaks because he is afraid of making mistakes. Sara knows fewer words, but she participates in discussions every day. She asks questions, shares opinions, and accepts feedback positively. After several months, Sara becomes a much more confident speaker because she practices regularly. Ali, however, improves more slowly because he avoids communication.

Another important aspect of communication is listening. Good listeners do not simply hear words. They pay attention to the speaker’s ideas, emotions, and intentions. They ask follow-up questions and show interest through eye contact and positive body language. As a result, people enjoy talking to them.

Communication also helps us solve problems. Families communicate to make decisions. Teachers communicate to explain lessons. Doctors communicate to understand patients. Business leaders communicate to motivate employees. Even scientists must communicate their research so that others can benefit from it. Without communication, knowledge cannot be shared.

Fortunately, communication is a skill that anyone can develop. Just like learning to drive, play football, or use a computer, becoming a good communicator requires practice. Every conversation, discussion, presentation, and interview helps us improve.

The next time you hesitate to speak English, remember one important idea: people are usually more interested in your message than your mistakes. Speak confidently, listen carefully, and continue learning from every conversation. Every great communicator was once a beginner

Dreams Become Reality through Goals

Many people dream about becoming successful. Some want to become doctors, teachers, engineers, entrepreneurs, or professional athletes. Others hope to travel the world, learn new languages, or start their own businesses. While dreams inspire us, they rarely become reality without clear goals and consistent effort.

A goal is more than a wish. A wish is something we hope will happen, but a goal is something we actively work toward. Successful people understand this difference. They break large dreams into smaller, achievable steps and follow a plan every day.

Imagine two university students who both want to improve their English. The first student says, “I want to speak English fluently someday.” However, he has no study schedule and rarely practices speaking. The second student sets a clear goal: “I will learn 20 new words every week, read one English article every day, and speak English for 15 minutes after each class.” After six months, the second student makes much greater progress because his goal is specific and supported by daily action.

Setting goals also helps people stay motivated during difficult times. Every journey includes obstacles. Students may become busy with exams, employees may face pressure at work, and athletes may experience injuries. Without a clear goal, many people lose motivation and give up. Those with well-defined goals are more likely to continue because they know what they are working toward.

Another important aspect of goal setting is flexibility. Sometimes life does not go according to plan. Unexpected challenges may require us to adjust our strategy, but we should not abandon our goal completely. Successful people review their progress regularly, learn from mistakes, and continue moving forward.

Researchers have found that writing down goals increases the likelihood of achieving them. People who write their goals often think more carefully about their plans and are more committed to taking action. Sharing goals with supportive friends, teachers, or family members can also provide encouragement and accountability.

Finally, it is important to celebrate small achievements along the way. Reaching a milestone, completing a difficult task, or improving a skill reminds us that progress is possible. These small successes build confidence and motivate us to continue.

Remember, success is not the result of one big decision. It is the result of many small actions repeated consistently over time. A clear goal gives direction, but daily effort turns that direction into reality.

The Power of Emotional Intelligence

For many years, intelligence was measured mainly by IQ, or Intelligence Quotient. People believed that academic knowledge and logical reasoning were the most reliable predictors of success. However, research over the past few decades has challenged this assumption by highlighting another essential quality: emotional intelligence.

Emotional intelligence refers to the ability to recognize, understand, manage, and respond appropriately to emotions—both our own and those of others. Individuals with high emotional intelligence are often better at building relationships, resolving conflicts, and making thoughtful decisions under pressure. Rather than reacting impulsively, they pause, evaluate the situation, and choose a constructive response.

Psychologist Daniel Goleman popularized the concept of emotional intelligence by identifying five essential components: self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, and social skills. Self-awareness allows people to recognize their emotional state and understand how their feelings influence their behavior. Self-regulation enables them to control emotional reactions instead of acting impulsively. Motivation encourages people to pursue long-term goals despite setbacks. Empathy involves understanding the feelings and perspectives of others, while social skills help individuals communicate effectively and build meaningful relationships.

Emotional intelligence plays a crucial role in both personal and professional life. In the workplace, managers with strong emotional intelligence are more likely to inspire trust, encourage teamwork, and resolve disagreements constructively. Employees who feel respected and understood are generally more motivated and productive.

In education, emotional intelligence contributes to academic success by improving collaboration, communication, and resilience. Students with strong emotional intelligence are often better equipped to cope with stress, receive constructive feedback, and remain focused on long-term improvement instead of becoming discouraged by temporary failure.

Fortunately, emotional intelligence is not a fixed trait. Unlike IQ, it can be developed through conscious practice. Reflecting on one’s emotions, listening actively, seeking feedback, and responding thoughtfully rather than impulsively are all practical strategies for strengthening emotional intelligence.

Ultimately, success depends not only on what we know but also on how effectively we understand ourselves and interact with others. In an increasingly interconnected world, emotional intelligence has become one of the most valuable skills a person can develop.

Can Money Buy Happiness?

For centuries, people have debated whether money can truly buy happiness. Some believe that having more money leads to a better life, while others argue that happiness comes from relationships, health, and personal fulfillment rather than wealth. So, who is right?

There is no doubt that money makes life easier. It allows people to meet their basic needs, such as food, housing, healthcare, and education. Financial security can also reduce stress because people worry less about paying bills or dealing with unexpected expenses. For individuals living in poverty, earning more money often leads to a significant improvement in their quality of life.

However, once people’s basic needs are met, the connection between money and happiness becomes more complicated. Research suggests that after reaching a comfortable standard of living, earning more money does not always lead to greater life satisfaction. Instead, people often become accustomed to their higher income and begin wanting even more. This phenomenon is known as the hedonic treadmill, where increased wealth quickly becomes the new normal.

Another important factor is how people choose to spend their money. Studies indicate that spending money on meaningful experiences, such as traveling, learning new skills, or spending time with loved ones, often brings greater happiness than buying expensive possessions. Likewise, helping others through charity or generosity can create a stronger sense of purpose and personal satisfaction.

Money can also create new challenges. Some people become so focused on earning more that they sacrifice their health, relationships, or free time. Working long hours may increase income, but it can also lead to stress, burnout, and loneliness. In such cases, financial success may come at the expense of emotional well-being.

This does not mean that money is unimportant. Rather, it suggests that money is a tool rather than the final goal. It provides opportunities, freedom, and security, but lasting happiness often depends on how people use those opportunities and whether they maintain a healthy balance between work, relationships, and personal growth.

Perhaps the real question is not whether money buys happiness, but whether people know how to use money wisely. After all, wealth may improve our circumstances, but our daily choices, values, and relationships ultimately shape the quality of our lives.

Living with AI: Friend or Foe?

Artificial intelligence (AI) is no longer a technology of the future—it has become part of everyday life. Millions of people use AI-powered tools to search for information, translate languages, write emails, create images, and even receive medical advice. From voice assistants on our phones to recommendation systems on streaming platforms, AI influences many of the decisions we make each day.

One of AI’s greatest strengths is its ability to process enormous amounts of information in a very short time. It can recognize patterns, analyze data, and perform repetitive tasks more quickly and accurately than humans. As a result, businesses, hospitals, schools, and governments are increasingly relying on AI to improve efficiency and solve complex problems.

However, AI also raises important questions. Some experts worry that increased automation may replace certain jobs, forcing workers to develop new skills. Others are concerned about privacy, since AI systems often depend on large amounts of personal data. In addition, AI can sometimes produce inaccurate or biased information, reminding us that human judgment remains essential.

Education is another area experiencing rapid change. Many students now use AI to summarize articles, explain difficult concepts, or practice writing. While these tools can make learning more efficient, they may also discourage independent thinking if students become too dependent on them. Rather than replacing teachers, AI should be viewed as a learning assistant that supports critical thinking instead of replacing it.

Ultimately, the future of AI depends on how people choose to use it. Like any powerful technology, AI can improve our lives when it is used responsibly and ethically. The challenge is not simply creating smarter machines but ensuring that humans continue to make thoughtful decisions. The most successful people in the future may not be those who compete with AI, but those who learn how to work alongside it.

Is Love Enough?

Love is often presented as the foundation of a successful marriage. People fall in love, imagine a future together, and sometimes assume that strong feelings will naturally carry them through life’s difficulties. However, marriage is more complicated than simply being in love. Two people may genuinely care about each other and still struggle to build a lasting relationship.

One reason is that long-term relationships require more than emotional attraction. Couples have to make decisions together, manage responsibilities, deal with financial pressures, and support each other during difficult periods. These challenges can reveal differences that were less obvious at the beginning of a relationship.

Communication is particularly important. Couples do not have to agree on everything, but they need to be able to express their concerns honestly and listen to each other. When disagreements are ignored, small problems can gradually become more serious. On the other hand, couples who discuss problems openly have a better opportunity to understand each other’s perspectives and find solutions.

Trust is another essential element. A relationship becomes difficult to maintain when one partner constantly doubts the other person’s intentions. Trust does not mean believing that your partner will never make a mistake. Rather, it means feeling confident that both people are committed to treating each other honestly and respectfully.

Another challenge is unrealistic expectations. Some people enter marriage believing that their partner should always understand them, make them happy, and meet all their emotional needs. Such expectations can place enormous pressure on a relationship. In reality, even healthy couples experience frustration, disappointment, and periods of emotional distance.

Successful couples also learn how to navigate conflict. Disagreements are unavoidable when two people share their lives. The important question is not whether couples argue, but how they deal with those arguments. Criticizing, insulting, or refusing to communicate can damage a relationship, while listening, compromising, and taking responsibility can help couples move forward.

Marriage can also be influenced by factors outside the relationship itself. Financial difficulties, demanding careers, family expectations, cultural differences, and social pressures can all create additional stress. Couples therefore need to develop the ability to adapt to changing circumstances rather than expecting their relationship to remain exactly the same throughout their lives.

Perhaps most importantly, a lasting relationship requires ongoing effort. The excitement of a new relationship may naturally change over time, but that does not necessarily mean love has disappeared. Emotional intimacy can develop in different ways as couples grow older. Spending time together, showing appreciation, supporting one another, and maintaining a sense of partnership can help keep a relationship strong.

So, is love enough?

Probably not.

Love may bring two people together, but trust, communication, respect, commitment, and mutual effort can determine whether they remain together. A successful marriage is not simply something people find; it is something they continue to build.

The World Is Getting Smaller

Imagine waking up in Kabul, checking a message from a friend in Australia, drinking coffee produced in another country, wearing clothes manufactured thousands of kilometers away, and then attending an online class with people from different parts of the world.

None of this seems unusual anymore.

A few decades ago, many of these experiences would have been difficult, expensive, or even impossible. Today, technology, transportation, international trade, and the internet have made it easier for people, products, ideas, and information to move across borders. This process is known as globalization.

Globalization has connected countries in ways that were once unimaginable. A company in one country can design a product, manufacture it in another, advertise it through a global platform, and sell it to customers almost anywhere in the world. A student can take an online course from a university thousands of kilometers away. A small business can reach international customers through social media.

This growing connection can create enormous opportunities. People can access products, information, education, and employment opportunities that were previously unavailable to them. Businesses can enter new markets and reach millions of potential customers. Scientists and researchers can collaborate across borders to solve problems that affect the entire planet.

Globalization has also changed culture. Music, movies, food, fashion, and languages now travel rapidly from one country to another. Someone in Kabul can listen to Korean music, watch an American television series, eat Italian food, and communicate with someone in Japan—all in the same day.

However, globalization is not entirely positive. Some people worry that local cultures and traditions may become weaker as global trends become more popular. Small businesses may also struggle to compete with large international companies. In addition, the benefits of globalization are not distributed equally. While some people gain new opportunities, others may lose jobs when companies move production to countries where labor is cheaper.

There are also environmental concerns. More international transportation means more fuel consumption and emissions. The production of cheap products for global markets can contribute to pollution and waste.

For these reasons, globalization is neither simply good nor bad. It is a complex process with both benefits and costs. The real challenge is finding ways to enjoy the advantages of global connections while protecting local communities, cultures, workers, and the environment.

Perhaps the world is not literally becoming smaller. Instead, distance is becoming less important. A person can now communicate, work, study, and build relationships with people thousands of kilometers away. The question is no longer whether globalization will affect our lives. It already does.

The more important question is:

What kind of globalized world do we want to create?

English: From a Local Language to a Global Lingua Franca

A few centuries ago, English was simply one language among many. It was spoken mainly by people in England and parts of the British Isles. Today, however, English is used in almost every corner of the world. People use it in international business, science, aviation, education, entertainment, tourism and online communication. But how did a language that began in a relatively small part of Europe become so influential?

The story is complicated. One important factor was the expansion of the British Empire. As Britain established colonies and trading networks around the world, English traveled with its institutions, administration, education and commerce. In many places, English became an important language for government and education.

However, the story did not end with the decline of the British Empire. During the twentieth century, the economic, political, scientific and cultural influence of the United States helped strengthen the international position of English. American companies expanded internationally, Hollywood films reached global audiences, and American universities and research institutions became highly influential.

Technology accelerated this process even further. As the internet developed, English became one of its dominant languages. International companies, researchers and technology professionals increasingly used English to communicate across national borders. A person in Afghanistan might watch a video created in the United States, read an article written by a researcher in Japan, and communicate online with someone in Germany—all using English.

But there is an important twist.

English is no longer controlled entirely by the people who speak it as a first language. Millions of people use English as an additional language, and they naturally adapt it to their own cultures and needs. English spoken in India may sound different from English spoken in Nigeria. Singaporean English has its own characteristics, while many other countries have developed their own varieties.

This has led to an interesting question:

Who owns English?

If most English conversations in the world take place between people who are not native speakers, perhaps English should no longer be viewed simply as the property of Britain, the United States, Australia or other traditionally English-speaking countries.

Instead, English may increasingly belong to everyone who uses it.

This does not mean that native-speaker varieties are disappearing. British, American, Australian and other varieties of English remain highly influential. However, the global spread of English has created a much larger linguistic community.

There is also a darker side to the story. The global importance of English can create advantages for people who have access to good English education while creating disadvantages for those who do not. English can open doors to universities, international jobs and information, but it can also become a barrier.

At the same time, some people worry about what happens to local languages when one international language becomes extremely powerful. If younger generations increasingly use English instead of their ancestral languages, some languages may gradually lose speakers.

Therefore, the global rise of English is both an opportunity and a challenge.

English can connect people who might otherwise struggle to communicate. But global communication should not require the world to abandon its linguistic diversity.

Perhaps the most interesting future is not one in which everyone speaks exactly the same English.

Perhaps it is one in which people use English to communicate globally while continuing to protect the languages and identities that make their communities unique.

Skill: The Schwa /ə/ — Making Unstressed Vowels Natural

One of the biggest differences between natural English pronunciation and careful learner pronunciation is the schwa /ə/.

The schwa is the weak, relaxed vowel sound found in many unstressed syllables. Learning to use it makes speech smoother, faster, and more natural—especially during presentations.

  1. The Schwa in Common Words

see how the unstressed vowel becomes weak:

about → /əˈbaʊt/

around → /əˈraʊnd/

support → /səˈpɔːrt/

today → /təˈdeɪ/

ago → /əˈɡoʊ/

perhaps → /pərˈhæps/

The first vowel in about is not pronounced strongly like the “a” in cat. It is relaxed: about → ə-BOUT

  1. The Schwa in Academic Vocabulary

These words are especially useful for today’s presentation topic:

globalization → /ˌɡləʊbəlaɪˈzeɪʃən/

communication → /kəˌmjuːnɪˈkeɪʃən/

international → /ˌɪntəˈnæʃənəl/

education → /ˌedʒəˈkeɪʃən/

linguistic → /lɪŋˈɡwɪstɪk/

professional → /prəˈfeʃənəl/

cultural → /ˈkʌltʃərəl/

different → /ˈdɪfrənt/

Students should notice that unstressed vowels are often reduced, rather than pronounced as clearly as stressed vowels.

  1. The Schwa in Function Words

The schwa is particularly common in weak forms:

to → /tə/a → /ə/the → /ðə/for → /fə/of → /əv/ or /ə/can → /kən/

Compare:

I want to learn English.

Natural speech:

I want tə learn English.

And:

English is a global language.

Natural speech:

English is ə global language.

The purpose is not to speak unclearly. The purpose is to make the important words stand out naturally.
