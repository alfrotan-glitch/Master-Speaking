## Unit 1 — original teacher-read active-listening script

**Status:** Original text authored for this companion. No pre-existing external video is claimed. Read the script at a natural, clear pace (or record it using a voice with permission). Let learners listen once for the overall message, then a second time for specific facts. Do not show the script before the task.

### Listening script: Making other people feel heard

Imagine that a classmate wants to tell you about a difficult day. You put your phone away, turn toward them, and give them time to speak. You are not trying to win an argument, deliver a perfect speech, or offer advice immediately. Your first goal is to understand what happened and help your classmate feel heard.

Good listening starts before the conversation. Find a place with fewer distractions. Silence notifications when possible, and prepare to pay attention. As the other person speaks, notice the main idea, not just the individual words. Do not spend the whole time planning what you will say next.

Questions can help. Open-ended questions such as “What happened next?” or “How did that affect you?” invite fuller explanations. A yes-or-no question can be useful, but it does not always give someone much room to express an idea.

Sometimes your attention drifts. That happens to everyone. Rather than pretending you understood, say, “Sorry, I missed the last part. Could you say that again?” Asking for clarification shows that understanding matters to you.

Finally, listening does not require agreement. You can listen carefully and still disagree respectfully. When people feel safe to explain themselves without being interrupted or judged immediately, they are more willing to share honest thoughts. Over time, that kind of listening can build trust and stronger relationships.

### Answers matched to the current Unit 1 listening questions

**Multiple choice:** 1 B (help the other person feel understood); 2 A (remove distractions); 3 B (open-ended questions); 4 B (ask for clarification); 5 B (trust and stronger relationships).

**True/False:** 1 True; 2 False — frequent interruptions do not automatically demonstrate interest; 3 True; 4 False — listening does not require agreement; 5 True — attentive listening can foster a psychologically safer discussion.

**Post-listening transfer:** In pairs, learners ask one open-ended follow-up question, paraphrase what their partner said, and acknowledge any misunderstanding without pretending to agree.

## Classroom fallback

For any interrupted or inaccessible stream, use a brief preview, postpone video-dependent scoring, and replace the exercise with a teacher-created listening passage or comparable licensed input. Never grade a student on video content they could not access. Students may always use a fictional or non-personal example for sensitive relationship, family, income, or emotional-health prompts.
